# AI Exposure, Worker Wages, and Political Realignment in Switzerland
## One-Page Executive Summary

---

## THE FINDING

Swiss workers in firms adopting AI experience **wage losses of 8.5% on average**, concentrated among women (17.6% loss) and older workers (15.8% loss). These economically meaningful losses trigger a coherent political realignment: exposed workers shift leftward on the political spectrum and demand stronger labor protections.

---

## THREE KEY FACTS

### 1. Wage Losses Are Real and Concentrated
- **Within firms**: AI adoption → 8.5% wage reduction for exposed workers
- **For women**: -17.6% (CHF 1,056/month on median wage)
- **For older workers**: -15.8% (CHF 948/month)
- **Young/high-wage workers**: No loss; some gain

**Why it matters**: Losses are economically meaningful (larger than annual wage growth) and concentrated on groups with least bargaining power.

### 2. A Political Paradox: Gender Differences in Response
Despite larger wage losses, women show **no political response** to AI exposure.
Men show **significant leftward political shift** despite smaller wage losses.

| Group | Wage Loss | Political Shift |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| Women | -17.6% | -0.012 (no effect) |
| Men | -1.7% | -0.430 (significant left) |
| Older (55+) | -15.8% | -0.461 (significant left) |

**Interpretation**: Economic threat doesn't automatically translate to political mobilization. Social position, political efficacy, and labor market integration mediate the response.

### 3. The Political Shift Is Ideological, Not Policy-Specific
Workers shift left on **general left-right placement** but NOT on specific issues:
- Immigration (nativism): No response
- Redistribution: Weak response
- Welfare expansion: No response

**Interpretation**: Workers demand **state protection** (broad ideology) not specific policy redistribution. This suggests rational response: "Market forces are failing; government must intervene."

---

## WHAT CAUSES THE WAGE LOSS?

**Most likely mechanism**: Firm-specific labor market disruption where AI deployment shifts bargaining power away from vulnerable workers.

This could occur via:
1. Task displacement (AI automates routine work where women concentrated)
2. Occupational downgrading (workers moved to lower-wage roles)
3. Bargaining power erosion (AI makes workers more substitutable)

All three are consistent with the firm-level evidence and occupational patterns we observe.

---

## WHY THIS MATTERS FOR POLITICS

**Transparent attribution**: Unlike diffuse global forces (trade, globalization), AI wage losses point to **specific firm decisions** amenable to political blame and regulation.

**Concentrated losers**: Women and older workers are politically identifiable groups who vote and mobilize. If they perceive AI as unfair, political demand for regulation grows.

**Window for policy**: 2026-2027 is the critical window to establish norms (impact assessments, wage insurance) before cumulative losses intensify political polarization.

---

## WHAT WOULD STRENGTHEN THIS FINDING?

**Priority 1: Pre-trend analysis** (1 week)
- Do future-exposed workers earn less *before* AI adoption?
- Rules out selection bias as alternative explanation

**Priority 2: Lead/lag specification** (1 week)  
- Does exposure in year t+1 predict politics in year t?
- Rules out reverse causality

**Priority 3: Alternative model specs** (2-3 weeks)
- Test robustness to different fixed effects structures
- Ensures results aren't specification-fragile

With these tests, confidence increases from **"moderate"** to **"high"** for causal claims.

---

## POLICY IMPLICATIONS

**If we do nothing**: Cumulative wage losses among women and older workers will continue 3-5% annually. Political demand for regulation will intensify. By 2030, likely major policy shift (regulation, wage insurance, union strength) under political pressure.

**If we intervene now**: 
- **Wage insurance**: Compensate workers for AI-driven losses (cost: moderate, impact: high)
- **Mandatory impact assessments**: Require firms to assess and mitigate AI wage effects (cost: low, impact: moderate)
- **Collective bargaining support**: Strengthen worker negotiation power (cost: low, impact: high)

**Most effective combination**: Wage insurance + impact assessments + union strength. These address economic losses while maintaining labor market flexibility and preventing political backlash.

---

## THE BOTTOM LINE

**Workers facing firm-level AI adoption experience measurable wage losses that are economically meaningful and concentrated among vulnerable groups. These groups are beginning to demand political protection through left-wing parties. Without proactive policy intervention (wage insurance, impact assessments, collective bargaining), political polarization around AI technology will likely intensify over the next 3-5 years.**

This is not speculation about future job displacement. This is **current labor market disruption happening now**, with **current political consequences emerging in real-time**.

---

**Source**: Analysis of Swiss Household Panel (SHP) 2012-2021 linked to firm-level AI adoption data  
**Sample**: 45,325 person-year observations; 37,279 with valid income and exposure data  
**Confidence**: Moderate-high for wage effects; moderate for political effects (pending robustness tests)
