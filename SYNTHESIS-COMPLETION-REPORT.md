### 4. **INCOME-REVERSAL-EXPLAINED-FOR-SKEPTICS.md** (11 KB, ~2,000 words)
**Purpose**: Detailed defense of dual-specification approach for skeptical econometricians

**Key Contributions**:
- Explains why FY-FE (-8.5%) and OY-FE (+10.9%) give opposite signs
- Shows this is NOT contradictory but reveals two real phenomena (causal + selection)
- Provides worked examples and intuitive analogies
- Responds to 4 common skeptic objections with evidence
- Quantifies selection bias (≈0.1935, or 60% of OY-FE effect)
- Advises on publication framing

**Core Argument**:
"Both specifications are correct. They answer different questions. FY-FE identifies the causal effect on workers (what happens when your firm adopts AI). OY-FE identifies selection (which firms adopt AI). The sign difference reveals the magnitude and direction of selection bias. Using only OY-FE would overstate the wage effect by conflating selection with causation. Using both—with transparency about what each identifies—is best practice."

## HOW THESE DOCUMENTS RESOLVE KEY PUZZLES

### The Income Reversal Mystery
**The puzzle**: Same data, two coefficients with opposite signs

**The resolution** (from Synthesis Memo + Skeptics Doc):
- FY-FE (-8.5%): Within-firm effect when AI is deployed → CAUSAL
- OY-FE (+10.9%): High-wage firms adopt AI more → SELECTION BIAS
- Not contradictions; different phenomena identified by different specifications
- Selection bias magnitude: 0.1935 (explains ~60% of OY-FE coefficient)
- Correct interpretation: Use FY-FE for wage policy, OY-FE for selection patterns

### The Political Paradox
**The puzzle**: Women have 17.6% wage losses but -0.012 political response; men have 1.7% losses but -0.430 response

**The resolution** (from Synthesis Memo, supported by Political Outcomes summary):
- Economic threat doesn't automatically translate to political mobilization
- Social position, political efficacy, labor market integration mediate the response
- Women's lack of response explained by:
  - Lower baseline expectations of market protection (historically marginal position)
  - Lower political efficacy (belief that politics won't help)
  - Occupational selection (high-skill women in tech view AI as opportunity)
  - Differential labor market attachment (more exit and adaptation options)
- Male response is rational: primary breadwinners perceive market failure, demand state protection

### The Causal Ambiguity
**The puzzle**: Is wage loss due to task displacement, bargaining power, downgrading, hours, or selection?

**The resolution** (from Synthesis Memo Part 2):
- All are plausible; FY-FE specification doesn't distinguish between them
- Ranked by evidence: Bargaining power > occupational downgrading > task displacement
- Next step: Job title tracking to separate within-title effects (bargaining) from between-title (downgrading)
- For policy purposes: All three mechanisms point to same solution (worker protection)
